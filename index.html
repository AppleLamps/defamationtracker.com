§ 01 — Opening

@ProjectConstitu defamation pattern: one in five dataset items flagged.

Most are not opinion. They are factual claims — verifiable in form, defamatory in substance, broadcast to 284,424 followers and amplified by hundreds of millions of engagements. Here is what the record looks like.

Between September 2025 and April 2026, the X account @ProjectConstitu grew from roughly 100,000 followers to 284,424 — a 184% gain in twelve months. Across 1,541 posts and replies, 321 items were flagged for potentially defamatory content, including 292 original posts and 29 direct replies. The pattern is not subtle. The account does not argue that Erika Kirk has been a poor steward of her late husband's organization. It states, repeatedly and across multiple categories, that she conspired in his murder, scheduled underage girls for Jeffrey Epstein, runs a CIA front, and had her credentials fabricated.

Each of those is a factual assertion about a private individual. Each is presented to the audience as established truth. And on at least one occasion — the wiretap, dated 14 March 2026 — the author publicly admitted he had identified the wrong person on a recording he claimed to have "verified." The retraction reached roughly 5% of the audience that saw the original. This site is what's left when you stop scrolling and read the record cold.

§ 10 — Legal Exposure

Why these claims matter under defamation law.

Informational analysis, not legal advice. The sketch below is drawn from black-letter defamation doctrine; the underlying facts in the record are what matter.

Defamation per se

All four common-law categories appear in the record: criminal conduct, professional harm, sexual misconduct, moral turpitude. Damages are presumed; specific monetary loss need not be proven.

Actual malice

The wiretap retraction is direct evidence the author claimed "verification" he had not performed. Continued publication after two cease-and-desist letters supports the second prong: reckless disregard for truth.

Massive reach

284,424 followers. 641 million engagements over the trailing year, +257% YoY. A peak global influencer rank of 2,796. The single false Epstein claim alone reached 11.7 million.

Two cease & desists

TPUSA sent at least two cease-and-desist letters during the covered period. The author published both publicly and continued. That is usable evidence of intentional, ongoing conduct.

Collateral Damage · Sentiment Capture

The campaign has measurably reshaped how the target is described.

Per LunarCrush's auto-generated topic summary for "Turning Point USA" (pulled 1 May 2026), the dominant critical themes in the public conversation are now "Erika Kirk Controversy & Event Criticism" at 40% and "JD Vance Criticism & Controversy" at 20%. Sentiment for the organization hit a one-year low of 28% on 30 September 2025 — within weeks of the assassination, as the smear campaign was peaking — and has only partially recovered to 53%. This is what reputational injury looks like quantified: a deliberately-published narrative becomes, over time, the public record of the entity it targets.

The "Allegedly" defense will not work.

The account occasionally hedges with "ALLEGEDLY" or frames accusations as questions ("Was She Hiding In a CIA Front Company?"). Courts have consistently held that this framing does not immunize defamatory content. Under the totality-of-circumstances test, what matters is the overall impression on a reasonable reader. When the surrounding text uses BOMBSHELL, SMOKING GUN, PROVES, DESTROYED, and EXPOSED — the impression is factual assertion, not speculation.
