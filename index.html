The Wiretap That Never Was

On March 14, 2026, @ProjectConstitu published the most viral tweet in the entire dataset. It was false. He admitted it. Here's the sequence.

Step 1: The False Claim

March 14, 2026 11.6M engagements

He claimed to have "verified" an audio file identifying Erika Kirk. He had not. The voice belonged to someone else entirely.

Step 2: The Doubling Down

March 14, 2026 (hours later) 7,036 likes
"FOLLOW-UP: The Erika Kirk Audio is REAL, and the context is even more DAMNING than we Initially Thought."
Before correcting, he amplified the false claim to his audience.

March 14, 2026 (later) 2,081 likes (5% of original)

The math is damning: 38,582 people liked the false accusation. Only 2,081 saw the correction. Tens of thousands of people still believe Erika Kirk was recorded scheduling underage girls for Epstein. She wasn't.

The "Allegedly" Defense Won't Work

The account occasionally uses "ALLEGEDLY" or frames accusations as questions ("Was She Hiding In An Office For a CIA Front Company?"). Courts have consistently held that this framing does not immunize defamatory content. Under the "totality of circumstances" test, if the overall impression to a reasonable reader is factual assertion, hedging language provides no protection. When the surrounding text uses "BOMBSHELL," "SMOKING GUN," "PROVES," "DESTROYED," and "EXPOSED"... the overall impression is overwhelmingly one of factual assertion, not speculation.
